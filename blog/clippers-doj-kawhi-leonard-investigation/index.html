DOJ Opens Criminal Investigation Into Clippers Over Kawhi Leonard Deal

Federal prosecutors in Brooklyn have opened a preliminary criminal probe into alleged sponsorship deals the Clippers used to pay Kawhi Leonard above the salary cap — a case that could escalate far beyond the NBA's own $30 million penalty.

By NBA GRID Staff • September 12, 2026 • 5 min read

The Kawhi Leonard salary-cap saga just moved from the NBA's league office to the federal government. The U.S. Department of Justice has opened a preliminary criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers over alleged sponsorship agreements reportedly used to funnel additional income to Leonard outside of his contract. According to reports, federal prosecutors out of the Eastern District of New York in Brooklyn are leading the inquiry, and at least one subpoena has already been issued.

The federal probe follows the NBA's own civil investigation and disciplinary action against the Clippers.

KEY TAKEAWAY

The DOJ inquiry arrives after the NBA already fined the Clippers $30 million, stripped the franchise of five first-round picks, and fined Kawhi Leonard $700,000 without suspending him — meaning the team now faces a second, potentially more serious, line of exposure.

From League Investigation to Federal Case

The NBA's own probe into the Clippers, led by outside counsel, centered on an alleged $28 million sponsorship deal between Leonard and the now-bankrupt firm Aspiration, along with a separate undisclosed arrangement involving Daktronics, the company behind Intuit Dome's videoboard. That civil investigation concluded with historic discipline: a $30 million fine, the loss of five first-round draft picks, and a $700,000 fine for Leonard. A criminal investigation by federal prosecutors is a different matter entirely, carrying the possibility of subpoenas, grand jury proceedings, and — if wrongdoing is proven — criminal exposure for individuals involved.

What Prosecutors Are Looking At

Federal prosecutors are examining whether the sponsorship arrangements were structured specifically to circumvent the NBA's salary-cap rules, according to reports, with at least one subpoena already issued as part of the preliminary inquiry. The investigation is still in its early stages, and no charges have been filed against the organization, its owner, or Leonard.

Why This Matters

A federal criminal investigation raises the stakes well beyond anything the NBA can impose on its own. It threatens to become an institutional crisis for the Clippers, adds legal uncertainty around owner Steve Ballmer's organization, and could complicate the team's ongoing effort to move Leonard, who has been connected to a potential deal with the Toronto Raptors. Even a preliminary investigation — without charges — can weigh on trade negotiations and sponsor relationships while it plays out.

WHAT'S NEXT

There is no public timeline for the DOJ's preliminary investigation. For background on the underlying NBA case, see our coverage of the Clippers' historic league punishment and the Daktronics sponsorship report.

  • Reported: Federal prosecutors in the Eastern District of New York have opened a preliminary criminal investigation into the same alleged conduct and issued at least one subpoena.
  • Pending: Whether the DOJ investigation results in charges against the organization, individuals, or no action at all.
