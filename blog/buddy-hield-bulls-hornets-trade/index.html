Bulls Land Buddy Hield From Hornets, Move On From Rob Dillingham

Chicago swaps a 21-year-old lead guard it barely got to know for a proven volume shooter already in his 30s. Charlotte, meanwhile, is betting on upside over floor spacing.

By NBA GRID Staff • September 27, 2026 • 4 min read

The Bulls and Hornets swung a straightforward, effect-immediately trade this week: Chicago sends Rob Dillingham and cash to Charlotte, and gets Buddy Hield in return. It's a swap of styles as much as anything — a 21-year-old lead guard the Bulls barely had time to develop for a 33-year-old who does one thing as well as almost anyone in the league, which is make threes in bulk.

Hield joins Chicago as the Bulls' new floor-spacing option off the bench or in the starting lineup.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Dillingham, the No. 8 pick in the 2024 draft, only landed in Chicago at last season's trade deadline and played just 30 games there, averaging 9.6 points and 2.8 assists. He's out the door less than a year after arriving. Hield, for his part, is on his second trade in five days — Atlanta shipped him to Charlotte earlier in the week before the Hornets flipped him to the Bulls.

What Chicago Is Getting

Hield's game isn't complicated, and that's the point. He's a career 39.5% three-point shooter who takes north of seven attempts a game from deep, the kind of gravity that opens things up for everyone else on the floor. The Bulls clearly decided they needed that more than they needed to keep developing a rookie point guard who hadn't had time to show what he could be in Chicago. There's also a money angle: Hield's salary runs about $3 million higher than Dillingham's this season, which is why cash is going back to Charlotte as part of the deal.

Why Charlotte Said Yes

For the Hornets, this is a bet on time. Dillingham is a 21-year-old with lottery pedigree who never really got a runway in Chicago, and Charlotte — already stocked with young talent — is betting it can be the franchise that figures out what he actually is. The Hornets also picked up a $9.6 million trade exception in the deal, which gives them a tool to use later in the season if another opportunity comes up.

Immediate Impact on Both Rosters

Both teams are circling the same target this season — a play-in spot, at minimum — and this trade nudges each roster in a different direction to get there. Chicago adds shooting and a bit more experience to a rotation that needed both. Charlotte trades a proven floor-spacer for a project, which is a harder sell in the short term but the kind of move rebuilding teams make when they're planning past this season rather than for it.
