2026 NBA Mock Draft: Consensus Big Board

A clear top tier has separated itself atop the 2026 class. Here's where the consensus stands on the top prospects and the biggest decision looming for the team with the #1 pick.

By NBA GRID Staff • September 9, 2026 • 6 min read
QUICK ANSWER

The consensus top four of the 2026 class is AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, Cameron Boozer and Caleb Wilson. The Washington Wizards, projected to hold the #1 pick, are mocked as choosing between Dybantsa and Peterson depending on the outlet.

This roundup reflects consensus across multiple published mock drafts, updated as the picture sharpens closer to draft night.

The Top Tier

  • AJ Dybantsa — widely mocked as a top-two pick, viewed by several outlets as the class's highest-upside wing
  • Darryn Peterson — the other half of the projected 1-2 conversation, mocked in the top two on nearly every board
  • Cameron Boozer — consensus top-four forward, seen as one of the safer, more NBA-ready prospects in the class
  • Caleb Wilson — rounds out the top tier, with mocks split on the exact 3-4 order between him and Boozer

This top tier has been the most stable part of the board — the guard-heavy group behind them has moved around far more from mock to mock.

The Wizards' Decision at #1

With the top pick projected to belong to the Washington Wizards, the entire draft's opening sequence hinges on one choice: Dybantsa or Peterson. Mocks genuinely disagree here — some outlets lean toward Dybantsa's two-way wing upside, others toward Peterson's more polished offensive game — and that split at the top is a big part of why this class has generated so much mock-draft traffic all by itself.

Who Else Is in the Lottery Mix

Beyond the top four, the lottery picture turns guard-heavy, which several mock drafts note could push additional frontcourt prospects further down the board than their talent level might otherwise suggest. Names that have shown up across multiple published boards in the 9-14 range include Darius Acuff Jr., Mikel Brown Jr., Brayden Burries and Yaxel Lendeborg, with Kingston Flemings and Aday Mara just outside that group on some boards. Expect real movement here as the college season plays out.

Why This Board Will Keep Changing

A mock draft in September isn't a prediction — it's a snapshot built from team needs, publicly available scouting reports, and league sourcing at a single point in time. Player stock in classes like this one can swing hard based on a strong or disappointing college season, a coaching change, or a lottery outcome that shuffles which team is picking where. Treat the top tier as the most reliable part of the board and everything after it as genuinely fluid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is projected to go #1 in the 2026 NBA Draft?

Consensus mock drafts have the Washington Wizards, who hold the top pick, choosing between AJ Dybantsa and Darryn Peterson at #1. Mocks are split on which of the two the Wizards prefer.

Who are the top prospects in the 2026 NBA Draft class?

AJ Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, Cameron Boozer and Caleb Wilson make up the consensus top tier across nearly every published mock draft, with a guard-heavy group filling out the rest of the lottery.

Is a mock draft an official prediction?

No. A mock draft is a projection based on team needs, prospect rankings and league sourcing from outlets like ESPN, NBA.com and The Ringer. Draft order and prospect stock shift constantly until draft night.

When is the 2026 NBA Draft?

The NBA Draft is traditionally held in late June, following the NBA Finals and draft lottery. An exact 2026 date should be confirmed against the league's official calendar closer to the event.
