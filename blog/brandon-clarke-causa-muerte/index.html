Medical Examiner Determines Brandon Clarke's Cause of Death

The Los Angeles County Medical Examiner confirmed the Memphis Grizzlies forward, who died at 29, passed from the combined effects of heroin and cocaine, in a death ruled accidental.

By NBA GRID Staff • August 7, 2026 • 4 min read

The Los Angeles County Medical Examiner determined that Brandon Clarke, forward for the Memphis Grizzlies, died from the combined effects of heroin and cocaine. The death was ruled accidental, according to the official report cited by ESPN and confirmed in a statement from the Medical Examiner's office itself. Given the sensitive nature of this news, this article limits itself to information officially confirmed by the relevant authority.

Brandon Clarke spent his entire professional career with the Memphis Grizzlies.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Clarke was found unresponsive in his bedroom around 5:00 p.m. on May 11. Paramedics pronounced him dead at 5:15 p.m. that same day, at age 29.

What the Medical Examiner Found

According to the statement from the Los Angeles County Medical Examiner's office, Clarke was found unresponsive in his bedroom around 5:00 p.m. on May 11. Paramedics who responded to the call pronounced him dead at 5:15 p.m. that same day.

The report also lists "the combined effects of multiple prescription medications" as an additional "significant condition" related to the death, though the office did not specify which medications.

Brandon Clarke's Career

Clarke was selected in the first round of the 2019 NBA draft and spent his entire professional career with the Memphis Grizzlies, where he stood out as a versatile, efficient defender near the rim. He entered the league out of Gonzaga University, where he established himself as one of the most efficient college players in the country.

Throughout his time with the Grizzlies, Clarke earned a reputation as a reliable rotation player, bringing defensive energy, rim protection, and highly efficient finishing near the basket.

The Organization's Response

While this article focuses on the information confirmed by the medical authority, it's worth noting that Clarke's death prompted an outpouring of grief from the Memphis Grizzlies organization, his teammates, and the broader NBA community in the weeks following his passing in May. Why the Report Was Made Public Now

Cause and manner of death reports issued by medical examiner offices typically require weeks or months of toxicological and pathological analysis before publication, which explains the gap between Clarke's death in May and the release of official findings in August. This is standard procedure in cases of sudden, unexpected death.
