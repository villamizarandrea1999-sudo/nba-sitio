Anthony Edwards Injury Update: Timberwolves Star Has Knee Surgery, Expected to Miss Up to Two Months

Edwards had a successful arthroscopic procedure on his right knee on Thursday at Mayo Clinic. Minnesota expects a full recovery but has not set a return date.

By NBA GRID Staff • October 29, 2026 • 5 min read

Minnesota Timberwolves star Anthony Edwards underwent a successful arthroscopic debridement on his right knee on Thursday, October 29. The procedure was performed by Dr. Craig Bennett at Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota, according to the Timberwolves' official injury update. The team said Edwards is expected to make a full recovery but did not give a timeline for his return. Sources told ESPN's Shams Charania that he could be sidelined for up to two months. Here is the latest on the Anthony Edwards injury, from what happened to his knee to when he could return.

LATEST UPDATE · OCT. 29, 2026
  • Surgery: Completed Thursday, Oct. 29, by Dr. Craig Bennett at Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minn. (official team release)
  • Procedure: Right knee arthroscopic debridement (a cleanup, not a meniscus repair)
  • Official status: Out; full recovery expected, no return date announced
  • Reported timeline: Up to two months, per ESPN. The Athletic reports the hope is a return in December.
  • Next update: The team says further updates "will be provided when available." We'll update this page as soon as they are.

What Happened to Anthony Edwards' Knee?

Edwards tweaked his right knee in Minnesota's season-opening win in Miami, according to the Pioneer Press. It is the same knee that bothered him last season, Charania reported. He was later seen grabbing the knee and grimacing during Sunday's home opener against Toronto, per ESPN.

Listed as questionable, Edwards still played 41 minutes in Monday's game at Cleveland. He scored a game-high 47 points on 19-of-34 shooting in a 127-125 overtime loss. Afterward, he said the knee felt "slightly different," which prompted more tests.

An MRI on Tuesday showed new damage that had not appeared in earlier scans taken during the playoffs and before training camp, ESPN reported. Timberwolves president of basketball operations Tim Connelly said the latest imaging "showed an issue that we want to get addressed quickly." Edwards was ruled out of Wednesday's game against Golden State, and surgery followed on Thursday.

Is It a Meniscus Injury?

No. Connelly declined to give the exact diagnosis but said, "it's not a meniscus." The team has described the operation as a debridement, which ESPN, citing Mayo Clinic, explains as a minimally invasive procedure to clean up or trim damaged cartilage, bone spurs or loose fragments inside the joint. Minnesota has not said exactly what was removed.

Connelly said the knee is not a long-term concern. He expects Edwards to be "good as new" when he returns. It is still the latest in a run of knee problems: Edwards dealt with runner's knee in his right knee last season and a bone bruise in his left knee during the playoffs, according to the Pioneer Press.

How Serious Is Anthony Edwards' Knee Surgery? What Doctors Say

The Timberwolves have not disclosed exactly what was treated inside Edwards' knee, so the details below describe the procedure in general terms rather than his specific case.

It is a minimally invasive procedure. According to the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS), knee arthroscopy uses a small camera inserted through small incisions to examine and treat the joint. Surgeons can trim damaged cartilage and remove loose fragments of bone or cartilage this way. Most patients go home the same day, and the AAOS describes the complication rate as very low.

Simple procedures tend to heal quickly. MedlinePlus, the health information site of the U.S. National Library of Medicine, notes that recovery depends on what was done inside the knee: simple procedures usually heal quickly, while more complex repairs can take several months or longer. It lists loose cartilage fragments among the problems that are often easily fixed.

Typical recovery runs from several weeks to about two months. Northwestern Medicine says arthroscopic debridement removes loose cartilage and smooths the joint surface, with a return to full athletic activity possible in as little as four to six weeks. The AAOS notes that many patients resume most physical activities after about six to eight weeks, while high-impact sports can take longer. Those general ranges are consistent with the up-to-two-month absence reported for Edwards.

The bigger question is long-term. Sports medicine physician Dr. Brian Sutterer told Yahoo Sports that this kind of procedure, with a roughly two-month absence, is not in itself a major structural knee injury. But he called it "a trade-off": removing damaged tissue also removes some of the cushioning that protects the joint, which can speed up wear and lead to further procedures down the line. The Timberwolves see it differently. Connelly said the knee is not a long-term concern.

How Long Will Anthony Edwards Be Out?

The Timberwolves have not announced an official return date. Their only formal statement is that he is expected to make a full recovery. The two-month estimate comes from reporting: sources told ESPN he could miss up to two months, and The Athletic's Jon Krawczynski reported the hope is that he returns in December.

A two-month absence would cost Edwards about 26 games, according to Sports Illustrated, which also noted that any setback in his recovery could push that back. Connelly said the team is "pretty confident" Edwards will return this season, and that Edwards is doing this "kicking and screaming" because he wanted to keep playing.

What It Means for the Timberwolves

Minnesota loses its leading scorer. Edwards' 133 points through four games set a franchise record, per ESPN, and the Timberwolves were 3-1 with him in the lineup. According to the team, he was averaging 33.3 points on 48.1% shooting, 4.8 rebounds, 3.5 assists and 2.0 steals in 33.8 minutes per game.

Head coach Chris Finch said the team will replace his scoring "by committee." The expected adjustments:

  • Ayo Dosunmu moves into the starting lineup, according to Sports Illustrated. He has averaged 12 points, 4 rebounds and 4 assists.
  • LaMelo Ball takes on more of the playmaking load after averaging 14.8 points and 6.8 assists through four games.
  • Jaden McDaniels and Terrence Shannon Jr. are in line for bigger roles, with Shannon as the main scoring option off the bench.

The 65-Game Rule and Edwards' Supermax

The absence also has consequences beyond the standings. Under the NBA's 65-game rule, players need to appear in at least 65 games to be eligible for major awards such as MVP and All-NBA. An absence of around two months would leave Edwards short of that threshold for the second straight season, the Pioneer Press reported.

That matters for his next contract. According to ESPN's Bobby Marks, missing the minimum would make Edwards ineligible to sign a four-year supermax extension worth about $301 million next offseason, and a five-year, $389 million extension in 2028. For now, Minnesota's focus is on the knee: "Our job … is to make sure our guy is healthy for as long as possible," Connelly said.
