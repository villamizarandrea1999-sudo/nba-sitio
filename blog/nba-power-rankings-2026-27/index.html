The Top 10

1. San Antonio Spurs (10-2)

Ten straight wins after an 0-2 start, the only unbeaten last-10 in the league. Victor Wembanyama has been the story: 52 points, 24 rebounds and 8 blocks against Dallas on Nov. 4, then 40, 11 and 8 in just 25 minutes at Phoenix. Next up is the Christmas Day Finals rematch with the Knicks.

2. Houston Rockets (9-3)

The best adjusted net rating in the NBA (+13.6 per 100 possessions). The 9-3 record undersells how thoroughly Houston has been beating people, and the formula rewards that.

3. Los Angeles Lakers (11-2)

The NBA’s best record at 11-2 and seven wins in a row, in their first season without LeBron James. The margin (+8.4 adjusted) is good, not dominant, which is why they sit third instead of first.

4. Portland Trail Blazers (8-3)

The surprise of the first month. An 8-3 record backed by a top-five net rating (+11.3), so this doesn’t look like a fluke built on close wins.

5. Minnesota Timberwolves (8-3)

8-3 and four straight wins with Anthony Edwards sidelined after knee surgery that could cost him up to two months. Up five spots from our preseason No. 10.

6. Boston Celtics (10-2)

The East’s best record (10-2) and 9-1 in the last 10. The schedule has helped: Boston’s adjusted net rating (+5.9) is well behind its raw margin, so it lands seventh here.

7. Golden State Warriors (8-4)

A +10.8 adjusted net rating, fourth best in the league, and 8-2 over the last 10 games.

8. Oklahoma City Thunder (8-4)

Last season’s No. 1 seed is 8-4 without Jalen Williams, who had surgery on a fractured fibula and ankle and will be re-evaluated before the end of the season. OKC still has the league’s best adjusted defensive rating.

9. Dallas Mavericks (7-4)

7-4 with three straight wins and a solid +6.7 adjusted net rating.

10. Detroit Pistons (7-3)

7-3 and third in the East by record. The raw margin (+8.1) is strong, but much of it came against weak opponents: adjusted, it’s +4.1.

How We Rank

A hot start against weak teams shouldn't be enough to reach No. 1, so we don't rank by record alone. Every team gets two ranks: one by winning percentage and one by adjusted net rating, Basketball-Reference's point margin per 100 possessions, adjusted for strength of schedule. We average the two and break ties with net rating. That's why Houston (9-3 but +13.6) ranks ahead of Boston (10-2 but +5.9), and why the 9-3 76ers sit 12th.

For every team's record and games behind, see the 2026-27 NBA standings, updated every morning.

Preseason vs. Now

Our preseason top 10, from September, looks very different a month in. Minnesota (No. 10 to No. 5) and San Antonio (No. 2 to No. 1) moved up. The Knicks (1 to 14), 76ers (3 to 12), Heat (5 to 19), Cavaliers (7 to 15) and Clippers (9 to 22) dropped. Boston (6 to 6) didn't move.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is No. 1 in the NBA power rankings this week?

The San Antonio Spurs are No. 1 in our Week 4 power rankings. They are 10-2 with 10 straight wins and the league’s second-best adjusted net rating (+12.3).

How are the NBA GRID power rankings calculated?

Each team gets two ranks: one by winning percentage and one by adjusted net rating (point margin per 100 possessions, adjusted for strength of schedule) from Basketball-Reference. We average the two ranks and break ties with net rating. The blurbs are our analysis.

Why are the Knicks so low?

The defending champions are 6-5 with a modest adjusted net rating (+2.7), middle of the pack on both counts. They started 0-3, won four straight, then lost to Cleveland.

How often are the power rankings updated?

Every week during the regular season, with the date of the update at the top of the page.
