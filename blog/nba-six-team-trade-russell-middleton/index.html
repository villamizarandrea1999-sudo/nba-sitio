NBA Six-Team Trade Sends Russell to Grizzlies, Middleton to Wizards

One of the largest trades in NBA history reshapes six rosters at once — headlined by D'Angelo Russell joining Memphis and Khris Middleton returning to Washington.

By Staff Reporter • July 7, 2026 • 5 min read

The NBA's summer got a lot louder this week. In one of the largest trades in league history, six franchises — the Memphis Grizzlies, Washington Wizards, Dallas Mavericks, Detroit Pistons, Milwaukee Bucks, and LA Clippers — combined on a single sprawling deal that reshuffled rotations across the board.

Six franchises, dozens of assets, one trade — among the largest in NBA history.

QUICK TAKEAWAY

The headline names: D'Angelo Russell heads to Memphis, and Khris Middleton returns to Washington for a second stint via sign-and-trade.

The Headline Move: Russell to Memphis

The Washington Wizards sent D'Angelo Russell, a 2029 second-round pick, and a 2033 second-round pick swap to the Memphis Grizzlies. In return, Memphis's side of the deal included Isaiah Stewart and AJ Johnson, along with a conditional first-round pick and multiple second-round picks, according to the Grizzlies' own trade announcement. Russell, entering his 12th NBA season in 2026-27, reacted to the move on Instagram: "No matter where you send me I'm still dangerous."

Khris Middleton's Return to Washington

The trade also completed a sign-and-trade that sends veteran forward Khris Middleton from the Dallas Mavericks back to the Wizards, where he began his career. Middleton will sign a three-year, $17.6 million contract as part of the arrangement — the first year fully guaranteed, the second year only partially guaranteed, and the third year non-guaranteed. It marks his second stint in Washington, returning less than six months after being traded away at the February deadline for Anthony Davis.

What Each Team Reportedly Received

  • Memphis Grizzlies — D'Angelo Russell, Isaiah Stewart, AJ Johnson, plus draft compensation
  • Washington Wizards — Khris Middleton via sign-and-trade
  • Dallas Mavericks — Santi Aldama and Marcus Sasser
  • Detroit Pistons — John Collins, Taurean Prince, and Gary Harris
  • Milwaukee Bucks — Caris LeVert
  • LA Clippers — Draft pick compensation

Exact asset breakdowns varied slightly across initial reports as details were finalized — check back as league sources confirm the full trade tree.

Why It Matters

Six-team trades are rare precisely because they require this many separate front offices to agree on value at the same time. For Memphis, adding a proven pick-and-roll scorer in Russell gives the Grizzlies more perimeter shot creation heading into 2026-27. For Washington, bringing back a battle-tested Middleton adds shooting and championship experience to a young roster still finding its identity.
