Misdemeanor Gun Charge Against James Harden Dismissed by Texas Judge

A Harris County judge dropped the charge against the Cleveland Cavaliers guard following the June incident.

By NBA GRID Staff • August 7, 2026 • 4 min read

A Harris County Criminal Court judge in Houston dismissed the misdemeanor gun possession charge against James Harden, All-Star guard for the Cleveland Cavaliers, according to ESPN and The Washington Post. The decision came after Harden completed an alternative resolution program, allowing him to avoid a criminal record.

The case was closed after Harden completed an alternative resolution program.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Harden was arrested June 13 in Houston after police found a firearm "in plain view" in his vehicle, a misdemeanor under Texas law.

What Happened and When

Harden was arrested June 13 in Houston after police found a handgun "in plain view" inside his vehicle. Under Texas law, having a visible firearm in a vehicle without the proper license is a misdemeanor, punishable by up to a year in jail and a fine of up to $4,000.

How the Case Was Resolved

Judge Alex Salgado of the Harris County Criminal Court dismissed the charge on Thursday, August 7, at the request of the Harris County District Attorney's office, following Harden's completion of the alternative resolution program. Such programs let defendants avoid a formal criminal conviction in exchange for completing requirements like community service. Court documents reviewed by ESPN don't detail the specific terms of Harden's agreement.

Context for Harden and the Cavaliers

The resolution comes as Harden is already part of the Cleveland Cavaliers roster, where he arrived on February 4, 2026, in a trade that sent Darius Garland and a second-round pick to the Los Angeles Clippers. In his final stretch with the Clippers, the guard had posted one of his best seasons in years, averaging 25.4 points per game.

What's Next for Harden

With the legal matter resolved, attention returns to the court, where Harden is preparing for his first full training camp with the Cavaliers, in a year when the franchise is also bolstering its roster with signings like Mario Hezonja and exploring further additions like DeMar DeRozan and Jonathan Kuminga.
