Every 6'7" NBA Player in 2026-27

6'7" is the most common height in the NBA this season. Here are all 61 players listed at 6'7", the best of them now and ever, and how LaMelo Ball turned the number into a meme.

By Davis Vance • Published November 5, 2026 • 8 min read
QUICK ANSWER

61 players on 2026-27 NBA rosters are listed at 6'7", more than at any other height. The best known include LaMelo Ball, OG Anunoby, Khris Middleton and the Thompson twins. The greatest 6'7" player ever is Julius Erving.

6'7" is the peak of the league's height curve: 61 of 526 rostered players. Chart by NBA GRID from team rosters on Basketball-Reference.

6'7" Is the NBA's Most Common Height

I counted every listed height on all 30 team rosters for 2026-27. That's 526 players, two-way deals included. No number came up more than 6'7": 61 players, or about 12% of the league. 6'5" was next with 57, then 6'6" with 56.

It's the median, too. Put every player in a line from shortest to tallest and the guy in the middle is 6'7", with 246 players below him and 219 above. The average lands around 6'6.8". So is 6'7" tall for the NBA? Not really. In this league it's about as normal as it gets. For the extremes, see our guides to the tallest NBA player right now and the shortest NBA player, or find out how many NBA players there are in total.

6'7" Point Guards and Shooting Guards

Most guys at this height play on the wing or at forward. A handful handle the ball, though. The point guards are Josh Giddey (Chicago Bulls), LaMelo Ball (Minnesota Timberwolves) and Anthony Black (Orlando Magic). At shooting guard you've got Dailyn Swain (Chicago Bulls), Gradey Dick (LA Clippers), Jaylen Wells (Memphis Grizzlies), Caris LeVert (Milwaukee Bucks) and Micah Peavy (Oklahoma City Thunder). When your point guard is 6'7", he sees right over the defense, and that's a big reason Ball and Josh Giddey can make the passes they do.

Why 6'7" Is a Meme Now: LaMelo Ball and "6-7"

Heard kids shouting "six-seven" and wondered what's going on? Blame a song. "Doot Doot (6 7)" is a track by Philly rapper Skrilla that first came out in December 2024 and got its official release on February 7, 2025. People on TikTok started cutting LaMelo Ball highlights to the beat drop (he's listed at 6'7"), and in 2025 those edits blew up.

After that it turned into a joke that could mean pretty much anything, all over TikTok and Instagram. Skrilla has said himself that there's no set meaning. Dictionary.com even made "67" its Word of the Year in October 2025. The basketball side of it is simple enough: 6'7" is LaMelo's height, and it's the most common height in the NBA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which NBA players are 6'7"?

61 players on 2026-27 NBA rosters are listed at 6'7", including LaMelo Ball, OG Anunoby, Khris Middleton, Amen and Ausar Thompson, Dyson Daniels and Brandon Miller. 12 of the 61 are on two-way contracts.

Is 6'7" tall for an NBA player?

Not by NBA standards. 6'7" is right in the middle: it's the league's median height and its most common one this season. Of 526 rostered players, 246 are shorter and 219 are taller, and the average listing is about 6'6.8".

Who is the best 6'7" NBA player of all time?

Julius Erving has the best case. Dr. J made 11 NBA All-Star teams, won the 1981 NBA MVP plus three ABA MVPs, and took home three titles (two ABA, one NBA). Rick Barry, Clyde Drexler and Paul Pierce are 6'7" Hall of Famers too.

Are Kawhi Leonard and Jimmy Butler 6'7"?

Not on paper right now. Both get called 6'7" a lot, but their 2026-27 roster listings say 6'6" (Leonard with Toronto, Butler with Golden State). That's why you won't find them on our list.

What does "6-7" mean, and what does LaMelo Ball have to do with it?

"6-7" comes from the song "Doot Doot (6 7)" by Philadelphia rapper Skrilla. TikTok edits paired the song with highlights of LaMelo Ball, who is listed at 6'7", and the phrase spread from there. Dictionary.com named "67" its 2025 Word of the Year.

What NBA player is 7'6"?

Tacko Fall, who is on a two-way contract with the Philadelphia 76ers, is listed at 7'6" on 2026-27 rosters, the tallest listing of any player attached to an NBA team this season.
