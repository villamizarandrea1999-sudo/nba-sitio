Jalen Williams Injury Update: Thunder Star Has Surgery on Fractured Fibula and Ankle, Re-Evaluation Set Before Season's End

Williams fractured his left fibula and dislocated his ankle in OKC's win over the Clippers. After surgery in New York, the Thunder say he will be re-evaluated before the end of the regular season.

By NBA GRID Staff • October 31, 2026 • 7 min read

Oklahoma City Thunder star Jalen Williams underwent a successful procedure on Wednesday, October 28, to address a fractured left fibula and reconstruct the ligaments in his left ankle after a fracture and dislocation, according to the Thunder's official medical update. The surgery was performed by Dr. Martin O'Malley at Hospital for Special Surgery in New York City. The team says Williams will be re-evaluated prior to the end of the 2026-27 regular season. Here is the latest on the Jalen Williams injury, from what happened to when he could return.

LATEST UPDATE · OCT. 31, 2026
  • Injury: Fractured left fibula and dislocated left ankle, suffered Sunday, Oct. 25, against the Clippers
  • Surgery: Completed Wednesday, Oct. 28, by Dr. Martin O'Malley at Hospital for Special Surgery in New York (official team release)
  • Official status: Out; to be re-evaluated before the end of the 2026-27 regular season
  • Reported outlook: Expected to miss months, if not the rest of the season, per ESPN's Shams Charania
  • Next update: We'll update this page when the Thunder share a new medical update.

What Happened to Jalen Williams?

Williams was hurt with 1:14 left in Oklahoma City's 108-101 win over the Los Angeles Clippers on Sunday, October 25, the Thunder's third game of the season. He was driving to the basket when his left leg buckled after contact on a dunk attempt, and Clippers guard Kris Dunn was called for a flagrant foul, Yahoo Sports reported.

Medical staff reached him almost immediately, and his ankle was covered with towels while he was treated on the floor. The game was stopped for about five minutes, and players from both teams looked stunned, according to the Associated Press. As he was taken off on a stretcher, Williams gave the cheering crowd two thumbs-up.

On Monday, Thunder general manager Sam Presti confirmed that Williams had fractured his left fibula and dislocated his left ankle and would need surgery. The procedure followed on Wednesday in New York.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's Reaction

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, the two-time MVP, covered his face with his jersey as Williams lay on the court, Yahoo Sports noted. In his on-court interview after the game, he struggled to find words. "Just feel bad for him," Gilgeous-Alexander said, according to the AP. "Nothing more than that."

He described Williams as someone who "just loves playing, loves the competition," and was clear that the team has to keep going: "We don't have a choice." He also made a point of saying that Williams is "not gone forever" and will "be back better than ever."

Coach Mark Daigneault was just as candid about the mood in the locker room: "We're not doing great," he said.

How Long Will Jalen Williams Be Out?

The Thunder have not given a timeline. Their only official guidance is that Williams will be re-evaluated before the end of the 2026-27 regular season, which is scheduled to end on April 11, 2027 (see our 2026-27 NBA schedule).

The reporting is more pessimistic. ESPN's Shams Charania reported that Williams is expected to miss months, if not the rest of the season, and Yahoo Sports wrote that he will likely miss most if not all of the campaign. Yahoo noted that the timing of the re-evaluation still leaves hope for a return in time for the playoffs.

How Serious Is a Fractured Fibula and Dislocated Ankle? What Doctors Say

The Thunder have not shared the specifics of Williams' rehab, so the information below describes this type of injury in general terms rather than his case.

A fracture with a dislocation usually needs surgery. According to the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS), ankle fractures that leave the joint unstable, including fractures combined with torn ligaments that let the ankle shift out of place, are typically repaired surgically, often with plates and screws, so the joint can be realigned and stabilized. Williams' surgery addressed both the bone and the ankle ligaments.

Bone heals faster than ligaments. The AAOS says broken bones generally need at least six weeks for early healing and 10 to 12 weeks to heal fully, while ligaments and tendons can take longer. Most people return to normal activities within three to four months, but sports take longer, and full recovery from some ankle fractures can take up to two years. Stiffness and swelling can linger for months, and arthritis can develop years later.

Williams' injury is part of a brutal first week for Oklahoma City. According to the Thunder's October 30 medical update:

What It Means for the Thunder

Williams played at an All-NBA level during the Thunder's 2024-25 championship run, as Yahoo Sports noted. Through three games this season, he was averaging 13.7 points, 4.7 rebounds, 3.3 assists and 1.3 steals.

His value goes beyond scoring. Yahoo's Kelly Iko points to his playmaking, shot creation and ability to guard multiple positions, and notes that Oklahoma City was plus-229 in nearly 2,000 possessions with him on the floor last season. With Wallace, his usual replacement, also out, Ajay Mitchell is the most likely to absorb his minutes, with Alex Caruso and Jared McCain also options for Daigneault.

Jalen Williams Injury History

This is the most serious injury of Williams' career, but not the first setback:

  • October 2022: As a rookie, he had surgery for an orbital bone fracture, Hoops Rumors reported.
  • 2025-26 season: He played only 33 regular-season games, missing time mostly because of hamstring problems and his recovery from right wrist surgery, according to the AP.
  • 2026 playoffs: He strained his left hamstring and missed six games, then left Game 2 of the Western Conference Finals against San Antonio with left hamstring tightness, NBA.com reported.
  • October 2026: Fractured left fibula and dislocated left ankle; surgery on October 28.
