Who Is the Shortest NBA Player Right Now?

Yuki Kawamura is listed at 5'7" and stands as the shortest player on an NBA roster this season — but he's still four inches taller than the shortest player in league history.

By NBA GRID Staff • Updated September 27, 2026 • 5 min read
QUICK ANSWER

Yuki Kawamura, now with the Chicago Bulls, is listed at 5'7" and is the shortest player on an NBA roster for the 2026-27 season. The shortest player in NBA history is still Muggsy Bogues, at 5'3".

Size has never been a hard requirement for NBA success — just ask the players on this list.

Shortest Active NBA Players Today

  • Yuki Kawamura — Chicago Bulls — 5'7"
  • Braden Smith — Indiana Pacers — 5'10"
  • Dennis Schroder — 6'0"
  • Jose Alvarado — New Orleans Pelicans — 6'0"
  • Tyus Jones — 6'0"

Heights are as officially listed by NBA teams and may vary slightly by source. Rosters shift over the season, so a few names here will move around as two-way contracts and trades happen.

Who Used to Top This List

The "shortest player right now" title changes hands more often than people expect, since it's tied to who's actually on a roster in a given season, not just who's short. Kawamura himself is a good example — he broke into the league with the Grizzlies before signing with the Bulls, and the shortest-active-player crown moved teams right along with him. Before him, that spot belonged to players like Markquis Nowell, who bounced between two-way deals and the G League without ever locking down a full-time roster spot, and further back, guards like Isaiah Thomas and Nate Robinson held the honor during their primes. None of that changes where the all-time record sits — Bogues has owned it since the late 1980s and nobody currently in the league is close to touching it.

The Shortest NBA Player of All Time

Muggsy Bogues is the shortest player to ever appear in an NBA game. Listed at 5'3" and 136 pounds, Bogues played 14 seasons in the league, mostly as the starting point guard for the Charlotte Hornets, and remains a cult favorite for proving that height alone doesn't determine whether a player belongs on an NBA floor.

Short Players Who Became Stars

Being undersized hasn't stopped several players from reaching the top of the league. Isaiah Thomas, listed at 5'9", made an All-NBA Team during the 2016-17 season with the Boston Celtics — the shortest player to ever do so. Spud Webb, also 5'7", won the 1986 Slam Dunk Contest over players nearly a foot taller than him, still the most famous example of a shorter player out-athleting the rest of the league.

Why Shorter Players Still Thrive in the Modern NBA

Today's NBA rewards speed, ball-handling and perimeter shooting more than any era before it, which has made room for smaller guards to carve out real roles even as the league's average height has crept up at other positions. Quickness on defense, tight handles in pick-and-roll actions, and the ability to shoot off movement all matter more now than a player's listed height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the shortest active NBA player right now?

Yuki Kawamura, now with the Chicago Bulls, is listed at 5'7", making him the shortest player on an NBA roster in the 2026-27 season. He played his first two seasons with the Memphis Grizzlies before signing with Chicago.

Who is the shortest NBA player of all time?

Muggsy Bogues holds that record at 5'3" and 136 pounds. He played 14 seasons in the NBA across four teams, mostly with the Charlotte Hornets.

Has a player under 5'9" ever made an All-NBA Team?

Isaiah Thomas, listed at 5'9", is the shortest player to ever earn an All-NBA Team selection, doing so during the 2016-17 season with the Boston Celtics.

Who is the shortest player to win the NBA Slam Dunk Contest?

Spud Webb, listed at 5'7", won the 1986 Slam Dunk Contest and remains the shortest player to ever win the event.
