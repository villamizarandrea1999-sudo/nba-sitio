Giannis Antetokounmpo Traded to the Miami Heat: The Full Breakdown

The Milwaukee Bucks sent their two-time MVP to South Beach in a blockbuster deal that reshapes the balance of power in the Eastern Conference.

By NBA GRID Staff • August 15, 2026 • 5 min read

The Milwaukee Bucks have traded two-time MVP Giannis Antetokounmpo to the Miami Heat, ending his 13-year run in Milwaukee and instantly turning the Heat into one of the league's biggest storylines heading into the 2026-27 season. It is, by on-court impact, the most significant transaction of the offseason.

Antetokounmpo joins a Heat roster that is now betting everything on an immediate championship window.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Milwaukee received four players, four first-round assets and a pick swap for Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis — one of the largest single-player trade returns in NBA history.

The Full Trade Package

In exchange for Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis, the Bucks received guard Tyler Herro, center Kel'el Ware, forward Jaime Jaquez Jr. and rookie guard Kasparas Jakucionis. Milwaukee also collected significant draft capital: unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, the No. 13 overall selection in the 2026 draft, a pick swap in 2030 and a 2033 second-round pick.

Trade Package at a Glance

  • Miami Heat receive: Giannis Antetokounmpo, Bobby Portis
  • Milwaukee Bucks receive: Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis
  • Draft compensation to Milwaukee: unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, the No. 13 pick in the 2026 draft, a 2030 pick swap, and a 2033 second-round pick

Why Milwaukee Moved On

The trade comes after the Bucks fired head coach Doc Rivers and concluded that their current roster was no longer built to compete for a championship around an aging supporting cast. Rather than run it back again, Milwaukee opted to reset, prioritizing a longer rebuild fueled by young talent and a stockpile of future draft assets.

Miami's All-In Bet

For the Heat, the deal represents a full commitment to competing immediately. Miami gave up rotation pieces in Herro, Ware, Jaquez Jr. and Jakucionis, plus a substantial haul of future picks, to build a roster around Antetokounmpo. The move signals that Miami's front office believes its championship window is now, not years down the road.

What It Means for the East

The trade immediately reshapes the Eastern Conference hierarchy heading into 2026-27, positioning the Heat among the conference's top contenders while the Bucks begin a new chapter built around draft picks and developing talent.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did the Bucks get for Giannis Antetokounmpo?

Milwaukee received Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Kasparas Jakucionis, along with unprotected first-round picks in 2031 and 2033, the No. 13 pick in the 2026 draft, a 2030 pick swap and a 2033 second-round pick, in exchange for Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis.

Why did the Bucks trade Giannis Antetokounmpo?

Milwaukee moved on from its championship core after firing head coach Doc Rivers, opting to rebuild around young talent and draft capital instead of continuing to build around an aging supporting cast.

Does this make the Miami Heat NBA title contenders?

Analysts widely view the move as an all-in bet by Miami to open a championship window immediately around Antetokounmpo, though the Heat gave up significant rotation pieces and draft capital to make the deal.

Did the Boston Celtics try to trade for Giannis Antetokounmpo?

Yes. CBS Sports reported the Celtics were involved in trade talks for Antetokounmpo but ultimately missed out, with Miami landing the two-time MVP instead.

What happened to Doc Rivers after the trade?

The Bucks fired head coach Doc Rivers before finalizing the trade, part of a broader organizational reset that led directly into the decision to move Antetokounmpo.
